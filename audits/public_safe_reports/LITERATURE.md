# Q1095 source and literature review

**Verdict, 27 November 2026.** High confidence that the result closes the stated bounded-support scale gap for the ordinary exact Laplace NPMLE. Bounded review found fixed-scale estimates and a weaker growing-scale threshold, with no matching primary result for the full maximizing likelihood at the cube-root boundary. Priority has not been established.

## Main source

Yifei Xiong, Nianqiao Phyllis Ju and Vinayak Rao, *Statistical Properties of Nonparametric MLE under Laplace Noise*, [arXiv:2608.25997v1, §7, PDF p. 14](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.25997v1#page=14). The [arXiv version history](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25997) and [author publication listing](https://nianqiaoju.github.io/publications/2026npmle/) led to the August 26 v1 used here. The source fixes a known support interval [−a,a], a known Laplace scale bₙ, and independent observations within each row of the experiment. The precise sufficient condition in Corollary 5.3 is bₙ=o(n^(3/16)/√log n). Theorem 5.4 compares point masses at opposite endpoints and rules out consistent recovery when bₙ is at least a positive constant times √n. The conclusion discusses the gap in the inverse-scale parameter εₙ=2a/bₙ and separately asks about support size of the NPMLE.

The new lower proof takes fixed laws with the same mean. The likelihood ratio differs from one by O(t²) in the two tails and by O(t) on the interior, which has probability O(t) for t=1/bₙ. The corresponding χ² distance is O(t³). The upper proof compares full maximized likelihoods, with a uniformly bounded-in-probability category contribution and a loss of order n t³ from the conditional interior shape for separated mixing laws. The theorem gives the exact consistency threshold and allows any exact maximizing choice.

## Related deconvolution results

- [Catia Scricciolo, *Bayes and maximum likelihood for L¹-Wasserstein deconvolution of Laplace mixtures*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.05445) studies standard fixed Laplace noise and rates for the sampling-density MLE and mixing law under its hypotheses. Rescaling the growing-noise experiment to its noise changes the true mixing support with n and divides the target Wasserstein error by bₙ. The fixed-scale conclusion alone gives no cube-root consistency theorem for the unscaled target.
- [Judith Rousseau and Catia Scricciolo, *Wasserstein convergence in Bayesian and frequentist deconvolution models*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.15300) give inversion inequalities and posterior contraction results for ordinary-smooth errors. The primary problem here is the behavior of the full likelihood as its geometry changes with bₙ; an inversion inequality alone does not provide the conditional shape separation and fluctuations needed at the stated boundary.
- [Jérôme Dedecker, Aurélie Fischer and Bertrand Michel, *Improved rates for Wasserstein deconvolution with ordinary smooth error in dimension one*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1404.0646) analyze Wasserstein rates for ordinary-smooth errors, with examples under fixed Laplace noise. Their estimates concern prescribed noise and do not state a triangular-array threshold for the exact full-data MLE.
- [László Györfi and Martin Kroll, *Multivariate density estimation from privatised data: universal consistency and minimax rates*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.12649) study a privacy channel obtained by adding Laplace noise to a discretization of the latent feature vector. The known scalar additive Laplace channel and the Wasserstein target here differ from that setting.

The direct search combined the source title and identifier with varying Laplace scales, the cube-root boundary, Wasserstein recovery and privacy. The result concerns consistency in probability over all fixed-support mixing measures. It does not give optimal convergence rates within the consistent regime, bounds on support size or claims for different privacy channels.
